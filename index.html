Deep dive · Lever 3

The Pronto Verified backlash is a trust-design problem, not a data problem. Fixing it protects a potential second revenue line.

01 · Problem

A small pilot became a sector-wide trust story

0.1%of customers were ever in the pilot1

Recording inside homes → MeitY reportedly took cognisance.1 Rivals now sell "we never record" as a trust feature, against Pronto.

Urban Company: "we do not" record inside customers' homes.2
Snabbit: "No customer's home has ever been recorded by us, in any way."2
02 · Real issue

The consent was built, but customers couldn't see it or follow it

One camera, two purposes.

Verified session — what customers thought they bought: quality and safety.
AI training data — a second purpose, sold to US AI and robotics companies.3

Surfaced via investor documents and a viral video, not from Pronto.1 Opt-in per booking, PII stripped3, but the second purpose wasn't obvious.

03 · Why solve it

The data line could rival the core business

$9–22M/yrfor every 1,000 usable hours/day

Derived: 1,000 hrs × 365 days × $25–60/hr, the 2026 benchmark for egocentric household-task video.4

Core house-help order value (est.)5≈ $24M/yr
Data line at 1,000 hrs/day$9–22M

Pronto already runs thousands of Verified sessions a day.3 Unresolved, DPDP penalties reach ₹250 cr, obligations from Nov 2026.6

04 · Fix

Make consent visible, separate the two purposes, and prove it

  • 1Split the product: safety-only "Verified" (no training, auto-deleted), plus a separate, paid data-partner program.
  • 2Share the value: discounts for customers who opt in, with full transparency that the discount is funded by the data program.
  • 3Make consent visible: notice before every session, camera indicator, one-tap withdraw and delete. DPDP-ready (Nov 2026).6
  • 4Prove it: third-party privacy audit, public transparency report, proactive MeitY engagement.

The goal isn't to stop Pronto Verified. It's to make Pronto the company that pays for household data openly and can show proper consent for every session.

Plan

Three moves, tested in 3 hubs over 90 days, would make micromarkets profitable and Pronto Verified sellable.

MoveHypothesisWhat I'd doSuccess metricWhy I believe it
1
Fill the idle hours
Utilisation
 Idle time falls in the midday dip between morning and evening demand peaks (to be confirmed with Pronto's hourly data). Split and peak-hour shifts; move scheduled and recurring bookings into the dip with a small price incentive; group jobs by building or housing society to cut walking time. Billed hours ÷ paid hours. Output +50%, cost −40%.
2
Pay for quality on top of guaranteed hours
Pro quality and retention
 The guaranteed shift keeps Pros from leaving, but it doesn't reward quality. Keep the guaranteed base and add a bonus linked to ratings and redo rate; Pro career tiers: Pro → Senior Pro → Trainer → Hub lead; send recurring, high-value customers to top-tier Pros; retrain automatically when ratings drop. Redo and refund rate; Pro retention at 90 days. Yield 42% → 63%.
3
Turn Pronto Verified into a data product
Data revenue · see deep dive
 Robotics labs pay for usable, consented, well-labelled footage, not raw video. A task list matched to what labs actually buy; quality checks on every clip (task completed, camera coverage, labels); a consent process ready for the DPDP rules; price per hour of usable footage; pilot with 1–2 labs. Usable hours/week; consent opt-in rate; revenue per session. 10k-hour robot program; 70% of pilots converted.

One extra booking per shift lowers labour cost per booking by ~15%. A shift costs ₹800–900 and covers 5–6 bookings, so each booking costs ≈ ₹155 in labour. At 6–7 bookings, that falls to ≈ ₹131. Illustrative, based on reported Pro pay and booking rates.
